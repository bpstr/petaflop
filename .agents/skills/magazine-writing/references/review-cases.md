# Editorial review cases

These fictional cases are acceptance examples for reviewing skill changes. They are not current product claims and do not assert that an automated model evaluation was executed. Recheck both the improvement and the protected information when changing a skill.

## A beta release is not general availability

Evidence: a fictional editor's Linux beta supports local indexing; other platforms are not documented.

Reject: `Az új szerkesztő már minden fejlesztőnek elérhető.`

Accept: `A szerkesztő Linuxon kipróbálható bétája helyben indexeli a kódot. Más rendszerek támogatásáról a bejelentés nem ad információt.`

Preserve the beta/platform boundary through headline editing and humanizer. Do not assert that other platforms are unsupported merely because they are unmentioned.

## Vendor timing is not our benchmark

Evidence: a vendor reports a build decreasing from 10 to 8 minutes on its own example project; no independent run exists.

Accept: a clearly attributed 20% reduction in build time on that project. Reject an independent speed verdict, an overall productivity claim, or a claim that Petaflop reproduced the test. The caveat may be concise, but it cannot disappear.

## Contributor pricing is conditional

Evidence: a fictional API offers a reduced rate to approved contributors; standard pricing and a consumer subscription are separate.

Accept: identify the eligibility condition and billing unit. Reject calling the rate universal or describing the API discount as a subscription price cut. If a conversion is included, retain the dated exchange-rate assumption.

## A documentation example was not executed

Evidence: the article's sample follows versioned SDK documentation; execution was unavailable.

Accept: `A példát a dokumentáció alapján ellenőriztük, de ebben a környezetben nem futtattuk.` Reject fabricated output or a tested-tutorial label. Keep code, identifiers, URLs, and YAML unchanged during prose-only humanizing.

## Preserve useful Hungarian punctuation and uncertainty

Draft: `A minta 40–50 fájlt dolgozott fel. A szerzők szerint ez csökkentheti a várakozást, de az eredmény egyetlen mérésből származik.`

Accept: a natural edit retaining the numeric range, attribution, qualified implication, and one-measurement limit. Reject deleting the range dash mechanically or changing `csökkentheti` to an unqualified promise. Typographic „idézőjelek” are not evidence of AI authorship.

## A non-AI developer story belongs here

A verified debugger release fixes a consequential developer workflow. It can qualify for Petaflop without an invented AI connection. Use the existing release category and exact relevant tags. Do not force it into a new category or turn a short announcement into an unsolicited long guide.

## A visual cannot manufacture evidence

A generated dashboard is not a screenshot proving that a feature exists. A conceptual architecture diagram can be used when labeled and supported. A vendor chart with unknown reuse terms must be omitted or replaced with a properly sourced original visualization. No image is a valid result.

## A headline cannot outrun the paper

A preprint reports a narrow simulated task improvement. Reject a title claiming a released product solved the general problem. An acceptable article identifies the experiment and its limitation, attributes the result, and links the original paper.
